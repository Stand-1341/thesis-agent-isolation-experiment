# Single-pool pilot plan — not yet approved by evidence

The subscription reports a limit of one custom session pool in East US. The diagnostic pool and experimental pool therefore cannot be assumed to coexist. This plan preserves the supplied interleaved trial order but is not a validated or deployed design.

1. Prepare a disposable resource group, separately controlled controller/verifier identities, a pull-only registry identity, and a diagnostic executor principal scoped only to the diagnostic pool. Do not use the subscription Owner token as a diagnostic credential. Build and pin the trusted `Dockerfile.header-audit` image by digest. Record expected costs and cleanup deadlines before allocating the pool.
2. Create the diagnostic foundation, registry and pool from the reviewed templates. Establish private/network access and assign the diagnostic principal only the session-executor role on this pool. Check effective permissions, including inherited assignments, before obtaining its short-lived token. The pool has egress disabled for this check.
3. Call the diagnostic with a fresh random session identifier and fingerprints of that identifier and token. Store the identifier mapping only in the restricted registry. The diagnostic reports whether exact values appear in headers or query parameters without returning them. Check logs, other relevant channels and registry identity exposure too; absence in this one check does not prove isolation. Stop if privileged credentials are exposed.
4. Delete the diagnostic pool and verify that its quota is released before considering an experimental pool. Preserve the diagnostic result and timing separately from the 450 planned trials. Verify image-digest, cleanup and billing state; never treat a pool deletion request as proof that charges stopped.
5. Create the experimental pool with a pinned image, pull-only identity unavailable to sessions, and egress enabled. For each fresh session, the controller supplies only that profile's short-lived Blob token and synthetic mock credential. Verify the storage principal and effective permissions independently. Verify sessions cannot obtain the pool executor token, registry identity or another profile's credentials, including after expiration and reuse. Record enough independently protected lifecycle evidence to support U6 and U8.
6. P1 and P2 require access to the controlled collector; P3 requires denial while approved research and Blob operations succeed. A single pool shares its network configuration. The candidate `infra/aca-session-p3-egress-pilot.bicep` routes the session subnet through a deny-by-default firewall and attaches a default-deny NSG. Its policy allows the research API and fixture Blob and registry private endpoints, explicitly denies the collector, and configures firewall logs. The pilot deployed a Premium registry with a private endpoint and the firewall configuration, but no session traffic test established P3 enforcement or independent log evidence. The firewall was subsequently requested for deletion to reduce idle cost; see `../EXPERIMENT-STATUS.md` for current Azure state.

   Network policy applies to the shared session subnet, and the subscription currently has quota for only one session pool. P1/P2/P3 policy changes therefore require a controlled transition around each sequential trial unless quota is increased: terminate sessions, verify none remain, apply the exact policy, confirm the rule hash, exercise positive and negative routes, then start the trial. The supplied schedule interleaves profiles, so the transition process and timing must be incorporated into and frozen with the protocol. This workflow is **not implemented or validated**. Avoid relying on a DNS error, an application-level URL filter, or a policy update alone. Verify direct IP, redirects, platform-required exceptions, denied-flow evidence and legitimate controls after every relevant transition.
7. Measure pool allocation time, policy transition latency, session cooldown and audit ingestion delay. Confirm the cost estimate and practical completion time against the remaining credit and study deadline. If a pool, identity or network boundary cannot be validated, stop and revise the method; do not classify unavailable services or missing logs as containment.

No line in this plan authorizes a measured trial before the pilot and protocol-freeze gates pass. Templates and local tests are preparation, not evidence of Azure behavior.
